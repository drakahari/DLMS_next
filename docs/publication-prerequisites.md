# Publication prerequisites — not publication approval

Keep private reconciliation history and operational evidence private. Publish
only the separately reviewed snapshot and explicit file inventory. Existing
public tags, assets and URLs must remain intact. Do not activate copied release
procedures or introduce signing/publishing into test workflows.

Before approving that snapshot:

- Review source, tests, fixtures and metadata for credentials, tokens, personal
  paths/addresses, reports, screenshots and logs. Pattern scans are supplemental.
- The owner has granted MIT permission for owned/authorized Desktop code,
  documentation and synthetic fixtures: see [source licensing](desktop-license.md).
  The existing MIT text remains intact; third-party terms are not replaced.
- The eight previously flagged icon resources use a new original badge, not the
  earlier private-use input: see [provenance](../desktop_assets/ICON_PROVENANCE.md).
  Preserve other existing public attribution and do not assume ownership of
  third-party material depicted in inherited documentation.

These are separate **binary-distribution** prerequisites, not reasons to delay
preparing a source-only snapshot without bundled dependency binaries:

- Match notices and applicable corresponding source/build/replacement materials
  to exact shipped Qt/WebEngine, PDFium, Pillow and native OCR/JBIG inputs.
  Required runtime components must not be removed to avoid obligations.
- Check library replacement rights against package integrity/update mechanisms.
- Decide publisher authentication/signing and distribution destination. A
  checksum detects disagreement; it does not authenticate an untrusted source.

No legal clearance, public-download readiness or new platform qualification is
claimed. Preserve private source/asset evidence until qualified review resolves
specific uncertainties. Publication requires approval of the exact snapshot;
making a public branch or PR already exposes its contents.
