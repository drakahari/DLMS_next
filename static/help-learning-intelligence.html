Learning Intelligence & Review Planning

Use tagged question evidence to decide what to review next.

Concepts connect questions to topics

Questions can carry small, reusable concepts, such as octal-permissions or file-ownership. Older compatible tags are accepted, but concepts are the current field. Use the same spelling when questions assess the same skill.

Untagged questions still work, score normally, and remain in History. They simply cannot contribute evidence to a concept-level topic, so they may not appear in mastery or weak-area views.

Choose your Learning Scope

Learning Scope answers “What am I actively studying now?” All folders start included. Open Manage Learning Scope from Topic Intelligence, Today’s Review, or Quiz Library to include or exclude a folder, including Uncategorized. The folder list also shows empty and hidden folders.

Excluded quizzes remain saved and playable. Their history, scores, concepts, and answer evidence remain intact, while current Learning Intelligence, Learning Profile, Diagnostics, Due Questions, Topic Retention, Adaptive Study, Smart Review, Concept Review, Today’s Review recommendations, and the Library Needs Review view leave them out. History, Analytics, missed-question review, and manual Mixed Quiz selection still cover the full Library. Hiding a folder only changes Library visibility; it does not change Learning Scope.

Generated practice follows each original source question’s folder. If you answer an excluded quiz manually, DLMS still saves the result. Schedules keep advancing with time; including the folder again uses the saved evidence, so questions may be due or overdue immediately. Unfinished Resume cards saved in this browser stay available.

How evidence follows a question

Study Mode records answer interactions. Exam Mode records completed attempt responses. Learning Intelligence combines this evidence across quizzes and Study Packs through each question’s saved concepts.

New generated practice keeps a durable link to its original source question. Results from Smart, Concept, Adaptive, Due Questions, and other generated sessions therefore strengthen the source question’s history instead of becoming unrelated evidence. Independently authored questions with identical wording remain separate; older quizzes without explicit lineage use a conservative compatibility fallback rather than guessed relationships.

If Study Mode cannot save an interaction, it shows Learning progress was not saved. You can keep studying and use Retry without resetting the current question.

Use enough evidence: one answer is a hint, not a conclusion. DLMS de-duplicates repeated interactions and waits for enough evidence before classifying a topic as weak or strong.

Understand concept performance

Open Learning Intelligence to see how many source questions and quizzes cover each concept, how many answers are recorded, correct and incorrect results, overall accuracy, recent accuracy, trend, and mastery.

Recent accuracy uses up to the five latest deduplicated answers. A trend appears only after at least six answers, when DLMS can compare two recent evidence windows. Smaller changes are shown as roughly stable; improving or declining requires a meaningful difference. Fewer than three answers remains Not enough data rather than receiving a weak or strong label.

Mastery is an explainable study signal, not a course grade or diagnosis. A low or missing value often means you need more tagged practice, not that DLMS has judged the topic permanently. Use How mastery works in the workspace for the current calculation.

Due Questions and Topic Retention Schedule

Due Questions is the individual question schedule. Independent first success in a new Study review advances through simple intervals only when the previous interval has elapsed. A first mistake or prior feedback/tool action keeps a short interval. Legacy Study and Exam results keep their existing scheduling interpretation. Unseen questions are listed as not yet scheduled until their first recorded answer.

Today’s Review shows the total number currently due. Its next Due Questions session includes up to 20 questions, or all due questions when fewer than 20 remain. For a different batch size, Review Schedule offers 10, 20, 30, or 50 questions and shows how that choice compares with the current due total. After a completed batch is saved, DLMS recalculates the remaining due count; start another batch if questions are still due.

The Topic Retention Schedule is concept-level. It estimates when a tagged topic needs reinforcement using mastery and elapsed time. Base mastery and retained mastery remain separate, so an overdue estimate does not rewrite recorded answers or accuracy.

Diagnostics and confusion patterns

Learning Diagnostics can surface repeated choice confusions and question-quality review signals. Treat these as prompts to inspect a topic or question, not automatic proof that content is wrong. Choice-confusion information applies only where answer choices make sense; matching and hotspot responses are not forced into that model.

What to do next

Start on the Dashboard with Today’s Review when you want DLMS to choose the most useful current action.
If you want a broad personalized mix, choose Study What I Need Most. If you want one known topic, choose its Study concept action.
Use Due Questions for individual scheduled questions and Topic Retention Schedule for concept-level timing.
Use History’s missed-question review for one completed attempt. If results look surprising across attempts, inspect Learning Diagnostics.

New Study evidence and historical coverage

New Study sessions preserve first graded outcomes and observable feedback/actions separately from corrections. Learning estimates use independent first success; a later correction does not erase initial difficulty. Review completion is coverage, not a passing prediction. Legacy evidence retains its older interpretation.

Study History survives a Learning Intelligence reset. Earlier facts do not silently rebuild reset estimates. Read the Study save, resume and completion guide.

Optional certification Exam Plans

Open Learning Intelligence → Exam Plans, then New exam plan. A plan organizes existing learning evidence; it does not predict whether you will pass.

  1. Enter the certification name and exam date. Check the Plan calendar timezone, initially suggested from this browser.
  2. Select exact folders. Folders are flat; there are no subfolders to include automatically. Plans respect Learning Scope exclusions. Hidden library folders are a separate choice.
  3. Choose study weekdays and estimated minutes per day. Expand Pace and review assumptions to adjust the starting estimates.
  4. Select Calculate / retry preview. Check unavailable content, remaining days and any shortfall.
  5. Optionally select Use this plan on my dashboard, then Save plan.

You can save several plans, but only one appears under Your exam plan in Today’s Review. Selecting a plan makes it selected and visible together. The form identifies the plan being replaced. Leave the checkbox unchecked to keep your current selection and visibility; ordinary edits do not switch plans.

Show, hide or pause a plan

On the plan page, expand Dashboard and plan controls. On dashboard identifies the selected visible plan. Hide from dashboard affects visibility; Pause suggestions stops this plan’s recommendations. Showing a paused plan does not resume it: choose Resume suggestions separately. None of these actions deletes learning history. If Today’s Review itself is hidden, use the link to Layout & navigation; selecting a plan will not override that preference. Plans do not send notifications by default.

What to do today

On the dashboard, View plan opens the plan. When work is ready, the separate Start suggested work button prepares focused practice directly. The plan page offers the same action. The first-pass/fresh-evidence target divides remaining coverage needs across remaining study dates, within your estimated daily allowance. Remaining capacity uses mistakes and due reviews, then other useful practice. A question that is both missed and due takes one place. Existing review intervals never change to force the plan to fit.

Each distinct eligible question answered today reduces the estimated budget. Refreshing does not assign a fresh daily quota; repeating the same question does not consume another estimated slot. Complete incorrect and assisted responses count as work. These are estimates, not measured active minutes. Optional extra practice offers Missed questions, Focused practice, Due reviews and Whole-quiz review, including optional work beyond the allowance. Extra work can use up today’s estimated allowance, but does not subtract time from future study dates. Generated practice does not replace Last regular quiz.

The ready suggestion shows its actual question count, estimated minutes and an exclusive breakdown. No recorded answer means DLMS has no identifiable saved answer for that question; it does not prove you have never seen it. Coverage / fresh evidence means it was answered before but needs current Study coverage or fresh evidence, for example after a question edit or learning reset. Other selected questions are grouped as Mistakes, Due reviews or Other practice. Overlap counts once: coverage needs take precedence, then mistakes, then due reviews. The counts describe the existing selection; they do not change its ranking.

If estimated work exceeds available study time, a visible warning offers Edit plan. Practice can be ready while the overall budget is short. The headline rounds the estimate; Why this? and the plan’s workload details retain the exact value and pace assumptions. It is a planning estimate, not measured time or a prediction of passing. Your last completed regular quiz remains available through the secondary Open current quiz link; unfinished Resume access remains separate.

Why this? explains the target and tradeoffs. On the plan page, Included material reviewed counts current reviewed questions among included material, including questions whose files or packs are unavailable. Excluded questions stay outside this primary denominator. The full selected-material total remains in Study evidence and history; exclusion is not completion. Expand Study evidence and history for first mistakes, corrections, assistance, partial work, full reviews, focused completions and independent success. Finish Review must be acknowledged. A correction does not erase first-response difficulty. A Learning Intelligence reset keeps factual coverage while separately requiring fresh evidence; old facts do not rebuild cleared mistakes or schedules.

When there is no suggested work

Read the specific status before changing anything. Missing, fully excluded or unavailable material needs attention first. The plan then checks the exam date, pause, selected study days and daily allowance. Other applicable restrictions stay visible. Follow the suggested edit or Learning Scope link; exclusions are never removed automatically.

Four questions answered today may leave four future reviews—about ten estimated minutes—without another suggestion today. Those minutes describe Estimated work remaining, not work due now or exhausted study time. The next review and study dates use the saved plan calendar. The review-due date follows spaced review; the next selected study day follows your chosen weekdays. They may differ, and neither changes the other. If a date cannot be determined, the plan explains what to check. Optional extra practice remains available when usable material is included; it never consumes future days’ allowance.

Dates, changing content and estimates

The exam date and weekdays stay in the saved plan calendar when you travel. Saved event timestamps display in your viewing browser’s local timezone. Capacity ends the day before the exam. Past dates, an exam today or no selected study days show zero capacity; edit the date explicitly if it changes.

Folder membership is live. Inspect additions, removals and edits under Changes to study material, then choose Mark these changes as seen. This action appears only when changes exist. A substantive folder rename requires Edit plan. Deleted folders do not automatically include Uncategorized. Material quiz edits conservatively make prior whole-quiz coverage historical; generated copies also need matching source revisions. Excluded or unavailable questions are not silently counted as finished. Study time available is your calendar budget; Estimated work remaining is a separate workload estimate. A useful workload estimate is unavailable when there is no included, usable material. Expand Estimate assumptions and uncertainty for the pace and sensitivity range.

Worked example: CISM

Suppose six study dates remain at 30 minutes each: 180 estimated minutes. Eighty unique outstanding questions at two minutes each plus a 25% review allowance require 200 minutes (100–400 with the starting sensitivity range). The base shortfall is 20 minutes; the uncertainty is substantial. The coverage target begins at 14 questions, but the budget fits only 12 at these assumptions. DLMS shows the tradeoff rather than promising all work will fit. Bringing the exam closer to leave only two study dates raises the required coverage to 40 per day, while the budget still fits 12; the base shortfall becomes 140 minutes. If you review five distinct first-pass questions today, only seven estimated slots remain; refreshing does not restart the twelve-question allowance.

In a 50-question Study review, 25 first wrong answers followed by corrections give 50 questions reviewed, 25 with a first-response mistake and 25 later corrected. Until Finish Review is acknowledged, the full review remains unfinished. Those factual counts remain after a Learning Intelligence reset; recommendations then require fresh evidence. A wrong answer followed by correction counts as reviewed and retains initial difficulty. It is due only when the existing schedule says so. Future errors, new content and additional reviews can increase work. A positive time balance is not proof of exam readiness.

Saving and recovery

Failed saves retain the prior saved plan. Conflicting edits ask you to reload. If practice generation loses its acknowledgement, use Retry same request; it returns the same quiz when publication succeeded. If publication was interrupted, keep the error visible and avoid starting replacement work while its outcome is uncertain. At the next normal DLMS startup, recovery checks its publication journal. Retry returns the original quiz if it was published. If recovery rolled it back, or its artifact was deleted, that request remains blocked: reload the plan and explicitly start new work only after recovery. Failed publication that fully rolled back immediately can use the same Retry button. Never clear browser storage to force a second request. Portable backups include plans; older backups contain none. Restoring invalidates stale plan pages, which must be reloaded.
