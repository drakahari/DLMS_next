Learning Intelligence & Review Planning

Use tagged question evidence to decide what to review next.

Concepts connect questions to topics

Questions can carry small, reusable concepts, such as octal-permissions or file-ownership. Older compatible tags are accepted, but concepts are the current field. Use the same spelling when questions assess the same skill.

Untagged questions still work, score normally, and remain in History. They simply cannot contribute evidence to a concept-level topic, so they may not appear in mastery or weak-area views.

How evidence is recorded

Study Mode records answer interactions. Exam Mode records completed attempt responses. DLMS uses the question’s saved concepts to associate that evidence with topics; it does not require extra tags in every learning event.

If Study Mode cannot save an interaction, it shows Learning progress was not saved. You can keep studying and use Retry without resetting the current question.

Use enough evidence: one answer is a hint, not a conclusion. DLMS uses safeguards such as de-duplication and minimum evidence before presenting a weak-area classification.

Mastery and weak areas

Open Learning Intelligence to review topic accuracy, mastery, recent evidence, and weak areas. Mastery is an explainable study signal, not a course grade or diagnosis. A low or missing value often means you need more tagged practice, not that DLMS has judged the topic permanently.

Use How Mastery Works in the workspace when you want the current calculation explained in the application.

Smart Review and Spaced Review

Smart Review selects existing tagged questions associated with weak concepts. Spaced Review selects existing tagged questions that are due on the review schedule. Neither workflow invents questions or changes your original source material.

Open Review Schedule to see due, overdue, due-soon, and fresh topics. Base mastery and retained mastery are shown separately so an overdue review estimate does not rewrite your recorded answers or accuracy.

Diagnostics and confusion patterns

Learning Diagnostics can surface repeated choice confusions and question-quality review signals. Treat these as prompts to inspect a topic or question, not automatic proof that content is wrong. Choice-confusion information applies only where answer choices make sense; matching and hotspot responses are not forced into that model.

What to do next

If a topic is missing, add consistent concepts to the relevant questions, then answer those questions in Study or Exam Mode.
If a topic is weak, open Smart Review or practice an existing Study Pack activity covering it.
If a review is due, use Review Schedule and complete a Spaced Review.
If results look surprising, inspect History for the specific attempt and Diagnostics for repeated patterns.
